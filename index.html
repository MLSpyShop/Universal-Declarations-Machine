PREAMBLE
We, the people of the world,
Recognizing that human beings have always sought to express shared aspirations
through principles, promises, charters, covenants, declarations, and visions of a
better world;
Recognizing that every generation faces circumstances that call for new expressions
of what ought to be protected, respected, cultivated, and shared;
Affirming that the power to articulate universal principles should not belong
exclusively to governments, institutions, experts, or established authorities;
Believing that people everywhere should have the opportunity to identify matters of
common concern, listen across differences, formulate principles, and declare the
future they wish to help create;
Respecting the profound diversity of cultures, histories, philosophies, religions,
communities, identities, and ways of understanding the human condition;
Affirming that universality need not mean uniformity, and that principles capable of
speaking to humanity as a whole may arise from many different experiences and
perspectives;
Recognizing that a declaration becomes meaningful not merely through words, but
through reflection, dialogue, implementation, accountability, and continued revision;
And recognizing the emerging capacity of digital tools and artificial intelligence to
assist people in thinking, organizing, expressing, translating, and sharing their
aspirations;
We proclaim this Universal Declaration of Universal Declarations.
ARTICLE 1
THE RIGHT TO DECLARE
Every person has the right to formulate and express a Universal Declaration
concerning matters they believe to be of significance to humanity, present or future.
No person shall be required to possess official status, institutional authority, wealth,
nationality, academic credentials, or political power in order to propose universal
principles.
ARTICLE 2
THE PURPOSE OF A UNIVERSAL DECLARATION
A Universal Declaration is an invitation to articulate principles, aspirations,
responsibilities, rights, commitments, or possibilities that may be meaningful beyond
the boundaries of a single person, community, institution, or nation.
A Universal Declaration may concern any subject capable of affecting human life,
human dignity, society, culture, technology, the natural world, or the future.
ARTICLE 3
UNIVERSALITY AND DIVERSITY
Universality shall not require sameness.
Universal Declarations should seek principles that can be understood, considered,
challenged, adapted, or embraced across differences while respecting the legitimate
plurality of human experience.
No declaration should claim universality merely by excluding those who disagree
with it.
ARTICLE 4
THE INCLUSION OF VOICES
The creation of Universal Declarations should strive to include voices that have
historically been unheard, marginalized, excluded, or underrepresented.
Children and future generations, where appropriate, should be considered.
People shall be encouraged to contribute according to their circumstances, abilities,
languages, cultures, and forms of knowledge.
ARTICLE 5
FREEDOM OF THOUGHT AND EXPRESSION
The creation of Universal Declarations is an exercise of imagination, conscience,
thought, dialogue, and expression.
People shall remain free to propose, support, question, amend, reject, reinterpret, or
create alternatives to any Universal Declaration, subject to applicable rights and
responsibilities.
No Universal Declaration shall acquire legitimacy solely through repetition or
assertion.
ARTICLE 6
DIALOGUE BEFORE DOGMA
Universal Declarations should invite reflection rather than demand unquestioning
obedience.
Those who create declarations are encouraged to ask:
● Who is included?
● Who might be excluded?
● Whose experience is missing?
● What assumptions are being made?
● What responsibilities accompany the proposed rights or principles?
● What consequences might follow from implementation?
● How might people from different cultures understand these principles?
A declaration should remain open to good-faith examination.
ARTICLE 7
HUMAN DIGNITY AND RESPONSIBILITY
Universal Declarations should recognize the inherent dignity of every person.
The assertion of rights should be accompanied, where appropriate, by consideration
of responsibilities toward other people, communities, future generations, and the
living world.
Freedom to declare carries a responsibility to consider the effects of what is
declared.
ARTICLE 8
PEACEFUL CREATION AND CHANGE
Universal Declarations should be developed and advanced through peaceful means.
Disagreement with an existing declaration is not, in itself, a failure of universality.
Constructive disagreement may strengthen understanding and reveal principles that
require clarification.
The peaceful creation of new declarations is itself a legitimate form of participation in
humanity’s ongoing conversation about its future.
ARTICLE 9
CULTURAL AND LINGUISTIC PLURALITY
Universal Declarations should be capable of crossing linguistic and cultural
boundaries without erasing them.
Translation, interpretation, storytelling, artistic expression, oral tradition, and other
forms of communication may all contribute to the creation and understanding of
universal principles.
No single language or cultural tradition shall be presumed to possess a monopoly on
universal insight.
ARTICLE 10
THE RIGHT TO IMAGINE THE FUTURE
Humanity has the capacity to imagine futures that do not yet exist.
Universal Declarations may therefore address emerging realities, technologies,
relationships, institutions, environments, and possibilities before they are fully
understood or widely recognized.
The absence of precedent shall not prevent humanity from declaring an aspiration.
ARTICLE 11
THE UNIVERSAL DECLARATIONS MACHINE
Recognizing the potential of accessible artificial intelligence to assist people in
developing and expressing their ideas, the Universal Declarations Machine (UDM)
is offered as a tool for this purpose.
The UDM may assist a person or group in exploring a subject, identifying principles,
considering audiences and consequences, incorporating cultural and ethical
perspectives, and drafting a Universal Declaration.
The UDM does not confer official status, legal authority, or universal legitimacy upon
any declaration.
Its purpose is to help people articulate their own visions.

ARTICLE 12
HUMAN AGENCY
Tools may assist in creating declarations, but responsibility for their meaning remains
with the people who propose, adopt, share, or act upon them.
Artificial intelligence, software, institutions, or other instruments shall not be treated
as substitutes for human judgment, lived experience, ethical deliberation, or
democratic participation.
The machine may help formulate the words.󳆫Human beings decide what they
mean.
ARTICLE 13
TRANSPARENCY AND REVISION
Universal Declarations should, where practical, make their origins, purposes,
assumptions, and significant limitations understandable.
A declaration may be revised when experience, evidence, dialogue, or changing
circumstances reveal a need for change.
Revision does not necessarily invalidate an aspiration. It may be evidence that
humanity continues to learn.
ARTICLE 14
DECLARATIONS AS SEEDS OF ACTION
A Universal Declaration should not be regarded solely as a literary or symbolic
object.
Its principles may inspire education, research, art, community action, institutional
change, public discussion, policy, technology, cooperation, or personal conduct.
Those who invoke a declaration are encouraged to consider how its principles might
become practices.
ARTICLE 15
THE RIGHT TO CREATE FURTHER DECLARATIONS
Every person and every community retains the right to create new Universal
Declarations.
There shall be no final Universal Declaration of everything.
Humanity’s circumstances change. Its knowledge expands. Its challenges evolve. Its
moral imagination develops.
The creation of one declaration therefore does not close the conversation.
It opens another.
ARTICLE 16
A LIVING LIBRARY OF HUMAN ASPIRATIONS
Universal Declarations may be shared so that people can discover, compare,
translate, challenge, improve, and build upon one another’s ideas.
Together, such declarations may form a living record of humanity’s attempts to
answer a fundamental question:
What kind of world do we believe is worth creating?
This library should preserve both agreement and difference.
ARTICLE 17
THE FUTURE IS NOT YET DECLARED
No generation can speak with absolute authority for all generations that follow.
Universal Declarations should therefore leave room for future people to reconsider
what the present generation believes to be universal.
We may declare our aspirations.
We may not declare that humanity’s thinking is finished.
ARTICLE 18
OUR COMMON INVITATION
We invite every person, community, organization, school, movement, institution, and
generation to consider what principle they believe deserves to be articulated for
humanity as a whole.
If something important has not yet been declared, let it be proposed.
If something has been declared inadequately, let it be improved.
If a declaration excludes those whom it should include, let it be challenged.
If a new possibility has emerged, let humanity find words for it.
And if no existing declaration adequately expresses the world we seek to build, let
us create another.
FINAL PROCLAMATION
We therefore affirm the right to imagine, articulate, question, refine, and share
universal principles.
We affirm that declarations belong not only to history, but to the living imagination of
humanity.
We affirm that universality is an ongoing conversation.
And we invite everyone to participate in that conversation.
